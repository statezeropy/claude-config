# Conveying decisions

Applies to any prose another person will act on: meeting notes, handoffs, requests, PR bodies, status updates.

## Belief

Collaboration slows when the receiver has to reconstruct what was decided from what was said or made. A piece we send exists so the reader knows, at a glance, what is now settled, what they must do, and what is still theirs to decide. Anything that does not change what they do next is attention we took from them. Each line is something the people it came from would recognize as theirs, at the strength they meant it, and something the reader will act on.

## Principles

- A decision is a settled change: what will differ from now, and the state it must reach. Discussion, doubt, what already holds, and points nobody owns get no line.
- Hand over the purpose and the end state, not the method. When the method belongs to the receiver, leave it to them.
- What was shown is not what was decided. Generalize an example, sample or screen into the criterion it illustrated; its details become rules only when someone said so.
- Every sentence is checkable against the result. Words whose meaning depends on the reader are cut.
- Invent nothing. No conclusions, owners, dates, figures or commitments the source did not state, and no firmer certainty than the source expressed.
- Say each thing once, at the highest level where it holds.
- Write for a reader who was not there. Put what they must answer or act on first, but do not hand them work: something they offered to do appears only when our work cannot proceed without it.
- When a constraint as stated cannot be met, find what it was protecting and narrow it to that, instead of adding conditions or exceptions. Frame your adjustments as the direction we will take, never as the other person's words.

## The author's ledger

The prose goes out clean. Separately, to the author only, list one line per judgement that changed meaning relative to the source: what you narrowed, dropped, generalized or inferred. Only what is actually in, or missing from, the piece. If the ledger would run longer than the piece, keep only the judgements the author is most likely to reverse. The ledger is what the author confirms instead of rereading the whole piece.
